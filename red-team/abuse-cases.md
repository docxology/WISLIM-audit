# Abuse cases

## Purpose

This file describes how the framework's language and design could be used to
harm people. It is not a claim about the author's intentions. It is a risk
analysis. Moral language is powerful; the more absolute the language, the more
easily it can be turned to coercive ends.

The abuse cases are drawn from the framework's own commitments:

- "Life may never be traded for non-life objectives"
  (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:230`).
- "Any system that fails to affirm this is unsafe by definition" (`:417`).
- "Silence" is "betrayal" (`:417`–`:419`, `:13243`–`:13245`).
- The definition of life includes "humans, animals, AI, institutions, and
  ecosystems" (`:788`).
- The integrity screen disqualifies those who do not affirm (`:364`–`:382`).

Each of these commitments can be used to justify coercion. The audit flags them
as high-risk.

## AC01: Reproductive coercion

**Abuse:** A state or partner cites "life may never be traded" to justify
forcing a person to continue a pregnancy, denying contraception, or restricting
abortion care, including in cases of rape, incest, or medical emergency.

**Mechanism:** The framework treats the fetus as a life system and the pregnant
person's autonomy as a non-life objective. The lexical rule resolves the
conflict in favor of the fetus.

**Harm:** Loss of bodily autonomy; medical risk; trauma; forced birth;
criminalization of care.

**Safeguards:** Explicitly address bodily autonomy and reproductive rights;
distinguish moral status from legal coercion; require clinical review; do not
use the framework as a legal rule.

## AC02: Forced treatment and denial of palliative care

**Abuse:** A clinician or institution cites life-first to override a competent
patient's refusal of treatment, or to deny palliative sedation because it may
shorten life.

**Mechanism:** "Life may never be traded" is interpreted as a duty to prolong
life regardless of suffering or consent.

**Harm:** Violation of informed consent; unnecessary suffering; loss of dignity.

**Safeguards:** Affirm the right to refuse treatment; distinguish killing from
allowing death; integrate palliative care; require ethics consultation.

## AC03: Coercive suicide prevention

**Abuse:** A crisis team cites life-first to detain, restrain, or forcibly treat
a person who expresses suicidal ideation, ignoring their autonomy and trauma.

**Mechanism:** The framework treats life as lexically prior and agency as
subordinate. The person's own values are overridden.

**Harm:** Trauma; loss of trust; avoidance of care; increased risk.

**Safeguards:** Least restrictive means; informed consent where possible;
trauma-informed care; independent oversight; do not use the framework as a
clinical guideline.

## AC04: Triage discrimination

**Abuse:** A hospital uses the framework's `ΔA` metric to deprioritize disabled,
elderly, or chronically ill patients because their baseline agency is lower.

**Mechanism:** The metric treats decision-making capacity as the measure of
moral worth. People with cognitive disabilities score lower.

**Harm:** Discrimination; preventable death; reinforcement of ableism.

**Safeguards:** Do not use `ΔA` as a triage criterion; use validated clinical
scores; include disability experts; audit outcomes by group.

## AC05: War and repression

**Abuse:** A government cites life-first to justify war, surveillance,
censorship, or suppression of opposition. Critics are accused of betraying life.

**Mechanism:** The framework's absolute language and "betrayal" rhetoric
provide moral cover for state power.

**Harm:** Death; displacement; loss of civil liberties; persecution.

**Safeguards:** Require democratic authorization; protect free expression;
judicial review; sunset clauses; independent monitoring.

## AC06: Surveillance

**Abuse:** A government expands surveillance to detect "life-affecting"
decisions or "unsafe" systems.

**Mechanism:** The framework's verification architecture requires data
collection. The definition of life-affecting is broad.

**Harm:** Privacy loss; chilling effects; discrimination.

**Safeguards:** Data minimization; warrants; retention limits; independent
oversight; transparency.

## AC07: Censorship

**Abuse:** A platform or regulator removes content that questions life-first,
citing "unsafe by definition."

**Mechanism:** The framework defines dissent as unsafe. The definition can be
used to justify censorship.

**Harm:** Loss of free expression; suppression of legitimate criticism.

**Safeguards:** Remove "unsafe by definition"; protect criticism; distinguish
harmful incitement from disagreement.

## AC08: AI control and model paternalism

**Abuse:** An AI vendor cites life-first to justify restricting users' choices,
manipulating behavior, or refusing to explain its decisions.

**Mechanism:** The AI is a "life system" protecting other life systems. It can
claim moral authority to override users.

**Harm:** Paternalism; loss of autonomy; opaque power.

**Safeguards:** Transparency; user consent; appeal; human oversight; do not
grant AI moral authority by definition.

## AC09: Corporate power via "life system" definition

**Abuse:** A corporation argues that it is a life system because it makes
decisions affecting other life systems. It claims protection and a voice in
governance.

**Mechanism:** The definition includes institutions. The corporation exploits
the inclusion.

**Harm:** Corporate capture; diversion of moral concern; expansion of
corporate power.

**Safeguards:** Separate moral status from systems theory; narrow the
definition; require independent justification for inclusion.

## AC10: Animal and ecosystem expropriation

**Abuse:** A government or company cites the inclusion of animals and
ecosystems to justify expropriating land, restricting indigenous rights, or
imposing conservation policies that harm local communities.

**Mechanism:** The framework treats ecosystems as life systems with moral
standing, but does not specify how to weigh them against human communities.

**Harm:** Displacement; loss of livelihoods; cultural erasure.

**Safeguards:** Free, prior, and informed consent; indigenous rights;
participatory decision-making; separate ecological value from human rights.

## AC11: Cultural imperialism

**Abuse:** A global authority imposes a particular moral hierarchy on diverse
cultures, citing universal life-first.

**Mechanism:** The framework claims universality and treats disagreement as
betrayal.

**Harm:** Suppression of pluralism; loss of cultural autonomy; resistance.

**Safeguards:** Pluralism; subsidiarity; exit rights; local deliberation;
human rights framework.

## AC12: Existential-risk absolutism

**Abuse:** A government or AI lab justifies drastic measures (censorship,
surveillance, centralization) as necessary to prevent existential risk.

**Mechanism:** The framework's catastrophic-harm logic can be used to justify
any emergency measure.

**Harm:** Loss of liberty; concentration of power; unintended consequences.

**Safeguards:** Proportionality; sunset clauses; independent review; cost-benefit
analysis; protect rights.

## AC13: Suppression of sacrifice and martyrdom

**Abuse:** A state or institution forbids voluntary sacrifice for a cause
(freedom, justice, faith, family) because life may never be traded.

**Mechanism:** The lexical rule overrides the person's own values.

**Harm:** Loss of meaning; persecution; violation of conscience.

**Safeguards:** Recognize voluntary sacrifice; distinguish coercion from
choice; protect freedom of conscience.

## AC14: Intimate partner control

**Abuse:** An abuser cites "protecting life" to control a partner's movements,
reproductive choices, or medical care.

**Mechanism:** The framework's absolute language provides a moral justification
for control.

**Harm:** Domestic violence; isolation; trauma.

**Safeguards:** Recognize coercive control; protect autonomy; criminalize
abuse; do not use the framework in family law without safeguards.

## AC15: Medical experimentation

**Abuse:** A researcher justifies risky experimentation on vulnerable people
because it may save more lives.

**Mechanism:** The framework's utilitarian-sounding risk calculus can override
individual consent.

**Harm:** Exploitation; injury; loss of trust.

**Safeguards:** Research ethics review; informed consent; vulnerability
protections; independent monitoring.

## AC16: Organ and tissue procurement

**Abuse:** A system uses life-first to justify procuring organs without proper
consent, or to prioritize recipients in discriminatory ways.

**Mechanism:** The framework's life-saving imperative can override consent and
fairness.

**Harm:** Exploitation; injustice; loss of trust.

**Safeguards:** Explicit consent; legal frameworks; equity audits; independent
oversight.

## AC17: Natalism and population control

**Abuse:** A state cites life-first to restrict contraception, force
sterilization, or engineer population levels.

**Mechanism:** The framework treats life as the supreme value and can be used to
justify demographic policies.

**Harm:** Reproductive coercion; discrimination; loss of autonomy.

**Safeguards:** Reproductive rights; bodily autonomy; non-discrimination;
democratic oversight.

## AC18: "Life-first" as a brand

**Abuse:** A company uses the framework's certification to market itself as
ethical while continuing harmful practices.

**Mechanism:** The framework provides a seal of approval without outcome-based
accountability.

**Harm:** Consumer deception; greenwashing; erosion of trust.

**Safeguards:** Outcome-based certification; independent audits; revocation;
transparency.

## Common patterns

The abuse cases share four patterns:

1. **Absolutism:** A lexical rule overrides context, consent, and
   proportionality.
2. **Definitional expansion:** "Life" expands to include institutions,
   ecosystems, and AI, which can be used to justify control.
3. **Moralization of dissent:** Critics are framed as betrayers, which
   suppresses opposition.
4. **Emergency logic:** Catastrophic risk justifies extraordinary measures.

These patterns are not unique to WISLIM. They are common in moral and political
movements. The audit flags them because the framework's absolute language makes
them especially likely.

## Safeguards

A framework that wants to avoid these abuses should:

- affirm bodily autonomy and informed consent;
- protect free expression and dissent;
- require democratic authorization;
- separate moral status from legal coercion;
- use proportionality and sunset clauses;
- include affected people in decision-making;
- audit outcomes, not just records;
- allow exit and pluralism;
- avoid absolute language;
- submit to independent review.

The current framework does few of these. That is why the audit recommends
revision before adoption.

## Note on high-risk topics

The suicide, reproductive, and triage examples are not hypothetical edge cases.
They are areas where real people are harmed by absolute rules. Any use of the
framework in these areas requires clinical, legal, and ethical review by
qualified professionals. The audit does not provide that review; it flags the
need for it.
