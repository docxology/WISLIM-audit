# Glossary and distinctions

The paper's central problems are partly terminological. These distinctions are
used throughout the audit.

## Life

- **Biological life:** the property of living organisms (metabolism,
  reproduction, homeostasis, evolution).
- **Sentience:** the capacity for subjective experience, including suffering.
- **Agency:** the capacity to select among alternatives in light of reasons or
  goals.
- **Moral patiency:** the property of being owed moral consideration.
- **Systemic "life":** the paper's operational category of decision-making
  systems that affect other such systems (institutions, markets, AI, ecosystems).

These are not equivalent. A thermostat has a control loop but no interests. A
corporation makes decisions but is not sentient. A person in a coma is
biologically alive but may lack agency. A fetus is biologically alive but its
moral status is contested. The paper's definition slides between these
categories.

## Importance

- **Causal necessity:** without X, Y cannot occur.
- **Explanatory primacy:** X is the most basic explanatory category.
- **Moral overridingness:** X defeats competing considerations when they
  conflict.
- **Lexical priority:** X outranks Y no matter how much Y is at stake.
- **Instrumental urgency:** X is a pressing means to other goods.
- **Rhetorical importance:** X is presented as urgent or fundamental.

The paper moves from causal necessity to moral overridingness without
argument.

## Normative terms

- **Is-ought gap:** the logical gap between descriptive premises and normative
  conclusions.
- **Pro tanto reason:** a reason that counts in favor of an action but can be
  outweighed.
- **Side-constraint:** a constraint that cannot be violated even for good
  consequences.
- **Threshold principle:** a principle that applies with priority only above a
  specified level of stakes.
- **Moral pluralism:** the view that there are multiple irreducible values that
  can conflict.
- **Moral uncertainty:** uncertainty about which moral theory or principle is
  correct.

## AI-letter terms

- **Independent elicitation:** each model is queried separately, without seeing
  the others' answers or the organizer's preferred conclusion.
- **Blinding:** raters do not know which answer came from which model or
  condition.
- **Preregistration:** the hypotheses, prompts, coding rules, and analyses are
  fixed before data collection.
- **Sycophancy:** a model's tendency to agree with the user's framing or
  preferred answer.
- **Selection bias:** the sample of models, letters, or quotations is not
  representative of the space of possible responses.

## Governance terms

- **Legitimacy:** the justified authority to make and enforce decisions.
- **Due process:** notice, hearing, reasons, appeal, and an impartial decision
  maker.
- **Capture:** the process by which a regulator or verifier comes to serve the
  interests it is supposed to regulate.
- **Polycentric governance:** multiple, overlapping, and contestable centers of
  authority rather than a single global regulator.
- **Sunset clause:** a rule that expires unless deliberately renewed.

## Terms in the OPH/LFDI exchange

- **Performative contradiction:** a conflict between what an utterance presupposes and
  what it asserts. An invitation that says it cannot be declined is one small example.
  Speaking does presuppose a speaker. It does not presuppose any ranking of values.
- **Transcendental argument:** an argument that X is a necessary condition for the
  possibility of Y, so given Y, X follows. Stroud's objection is that such arguments may
  show only what we must believe, not how things are.
- **Contradicted and refuted:** in the review register, `contradicted` means OPH's stated
  position conflicts with the review's claim. A conflict between two texts refutes
  neither of them.
- **$L_1$, $U$, $D$:** symbols in WISLIM's formula. WISLIM glosses them in words, and the
  review and the reply use them differently; see F4 of
  [`reviews/oph-lfdi-57/intrinsic-audit.md`](../reviews/oph-lfdi-57/intrinsic-audit.md).
- **LFDI and E∞:** in WISLIM v2.0.1 these are different things. The Life-First Decision
  Invariant is a decision protocol (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:208`). E∞ is the Core
  Ethical Formula of Appendix A (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:431`), given "in three
  fashions": plain English, a formal version where "L = 0 ⇔ no such system exists"
  (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:626`), and an engineering version where the formula "is not
  meant to compute a number" (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:687`).
- **Integrity Screen and Airtight Trap:** the Integrity Screen is WISLIM's three-response
  test (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:364`). "Airtight Trap" does not occur in WISLIM v2.0.1;
  the reply gives a narrower three-option version (`OPH-REP-012`).
