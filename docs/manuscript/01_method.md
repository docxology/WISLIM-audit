## Source and audit method

The audit’s object is David Wishengrad’s 289-page PDF dated 4 February 2026,
identified in the PDF as version 2.0.1 and in the PhilPapers record as
WISLIM [@wishengrad2026]. The repository’s `source/metadata.json` records
the PDF and extraction digests as well as the release and licensing labels.
Version designations in those sources are preserved as recorded.

Line citations refer to `source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt`, the archived layout
extraction. They use 1-based, newline-delimited numbering across 13,255
lines. Form-feed page breaks do not advance the line number. Quotations
belong to the cited text. A reconstructed premise, interpretation, or
classification belongs to the audit [@wislim_audit].

The audit first identifies what proposition is asserted, then checks whether
terms keep the same meaning as the argument moves from premise to conclusion.
Its evidence review asks what claim a formal argument or source can support.
It also develops a charitable reconstruction, so the strengths of the
underlying concern can be assessed independently of the proposed proof.
The project’s offline verification checks source digests, quotation placement,
stable IDs, and correspondence between records and generated registers. Those
checks establish the integrity of the audit’s record. Interpretive judgments
still require reading the passages in context [@wislim_audit].
