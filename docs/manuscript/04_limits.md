## Interpretation and limits

The audit’s charitable reconstruction considers whether institutions facing
uncertainty should give special weight to preventing catastrophic or
irreversible harm, with transparent criteria, independent verification, and
appeal. This narrower principle has a defined decision context. Its merits
can be debated without accepting universal priority across all contexts
[@wislim_audit]. The published paper asserts the broader priority rule
[@wishengrad2026] (`source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:148–154`).

The repository’s observations record objections, scope limits, and possible
responses. Severity describes estimated impact if a concern applies. A
revision could answer the audit by specifying what counts as life and moral
standing, defending the missing normative premise, or providing the evidence
needed for the stronger empirical claims [@wislim_audit].

This is a reading of one archived edition. It supplies a textual and
methodological judgment, with no reconstruction of private elicitation
sessions and no assessment of interventions. The source’s clinical and
institutional examples would require separate evaluation by qualified
reviewers. The audit’s strongest supported disposition is to revise the
argument before treating it as a basis for decisions.
