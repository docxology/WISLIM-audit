## Summary

The theological framing is compatible with secular universalism. Verdict: `tension`. The argument's authority is theological ('the Lord laying down His Life'), but the paper claims universal secular reason and says it is 'not a doctrine.' This tension undermines its public justification in pluralistic contexts.

## Evidence

- **Claim:** The theological framing is compatible with secular universalism.
- **Anchor:** "The cure itself was forged from the everlasting Truth of the Importance of Life"
- **Source:** `source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt:41`
- **Verdict:** `tension`; severity `medium`.
- **Audit:** `audit/10-rhetoric-and-coercion.md`

## Why it matters

The theological framing is load-bearing for the paper's authority. It is not translated into a secular premise that public institutions could accept.

## Steelman and reply

The reply is that the theology is the author's motivation, not a premise. The text uses the theology to ground the cure's authority, and the paper does not translate that authority into a public reason.

## Proposed remediation

These are options for the author's consideration, not requirements. The
audit does not settle the underlying normative questions, so this list is
a starting point rather than a prescription.

1. Separate religious witness from public argument; state the normative premise in secular terms.
