## Review claim

> Without $L_1$, every competing candidate for value, truth, or optimization within the OPH framework becomes undefined and non-applicable.
>
> — `reviews/oph-lfdi-57/source/LFDI_Alignment_Audit-57.txt:4`

## Intrinsic assessment

This is a conditional conclusion presented as a refutation. It assumes LFDI's ontology and metric, then reports that OPH fails by that metric. That can be a valid external critique if the metric is defended, but the review does not defend it. It also treats 'value, truth, or optimization' as one class, conflating descriptive and normative domains.

## Extrinsic assessment

OPH addresses observer definition, life, fixed points, and physics/ethics separation. It does not mention LFDI or WISLIM; it therefore does not accept the review's premises. The review's 'terminal failure' is not established on OPH's own terms. It is better read as a conditional normative objection: 'If LFDI is granted, OPH lacks L1.'

## Summary

The conclusion is framework-relative: it follows only if one grants LFDI's undefined variables and its priority. OPH does not, and the review does not show that OPH's own results fail.

## Suggested response
