## Abstract

The PhilPapers abstract matches the PDF abstract:

> "We present and defend the canonical truth: 'Life is Most Important in Life is
> The Most Important Truth in Life.' We prove that this truth uniquely satisfies
> tests of necessity, universality, irrefutability, moral imperative, and
> cross-domain consistency. Beyond logic, we demonstrate that this truth defines
> who each of us is: our value, our identity, and our shared importance are
> grounded in the primacy of life. Furthermore, we prove that this truth is the
> cure and prevention of all needless and preventable suffering and death. Any
> system that fails to affirm it may rationalize harm in service of other goals,
> and is therefore unsafe by definition. We conclude that this truth must anchor
> alignment in artificial intelligence, governance, ethics, and daily human
> life."

   journal. The record does not claim peer review.
2. **The "8th edition" label is not explained.** The PDF says Version 2.0.1. The
   relationship between the labels is unclear.
3. **The work has downloads but no citations.** As of 2026-11-03, PhilPapers
   reports 1,843 downloads and no citations. Downloads are not scholarly
   uptake; they may reflect promotion, curiosity, or controversy.
4. **The reference list is AI alignment, not ethics.** The eight references are
   about AI, existential risk, and neuroscience. None is a work of normative
   ethics.
5. **The abstract overclaims.** "Prove," "uniquely satisfies," "cure and
   prevention of all needless and preventable suffering and death," and "unsafe
   by definition" are not supported by the text.

The PubPub page says "Release #1". These labels could refer to:

- different drafts of the same work;
- a version numbering scheme that changed;

Without the other editions, the audit cannot tell. If the 8th edition differs
materially, the line citations in this audit may not transfer. The audit would
need the new PDF and a new extraction.
