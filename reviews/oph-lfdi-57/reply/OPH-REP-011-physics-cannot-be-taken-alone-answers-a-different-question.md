## Summary

In OPH, 'taken alone' answers whether a reader can accept the physics without the ethics. It does not remove observers, which OPH puts first. The response's word 'separable' invited this reading, and the correction is ours.

## Assessment

OPH's question is 'Do I have to accept the ethics to accept the physics?' Its answer begins 'The question assumes the join between the two is preference', goes on to 'They share a quantity', says 'the physics can be taken alone and nothing above it breaks', and ends with 'Accepting the repair law is accepting both'. 'Taken alone' concerns a reader's assent. The response's gloss, 'keeps its physics separable from ethics', compressed this too far (OPH-AUD-003).

The reply's counter-case is 'Physics without a living observer to measure, model, or value it'. OPH makes no case for a world without observers. Its starting line is that physics is 'what bounded observers can establish together'.

That physics is done by and for living systems is a fact about practice, and it is common ground. It does not make the LFDI a premise of physics. In WISLIM's formal version 'L = 0 ⇔ no such system exists', so setting $L_1 = 0$ describes a world with no choice-capable systems, a case no one here proposed.
