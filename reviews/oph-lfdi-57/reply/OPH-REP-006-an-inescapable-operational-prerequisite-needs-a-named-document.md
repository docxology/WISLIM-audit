## Summary

'LFDI Engineering Proof' names no document that has been supplied. The nearest thing in WISLIM v2.0.1 is its 'Engineering and Practical Version', which says something different. 'Epistemic Invariant', 'No Truth Outside', 'Airtight', and 'Decision Entropy' do not occur in that edition. The response's author does abide by inescapable constraints; those constraints do not rank truths.

## Assessment

A term search of `source/WISLIM-v2.0.1.txt` returns zero hits for 'Engineering Proof', 'Airtight', 'Epistemic Invariant', 'No Truth Outside', 'Decision Entropy', and 'Performative'. The nearest passages are 'Life-First Framework — Engineering and Practical Version (Version 3)', the Integrity Screen, and 'ethical entropy (D)'. A public-web search on 2026-11-03 found 'Epistemic Invariant' in a forum post crediting WISLIM, not in a document presented as this proof (linked below). To evaluate a proof, a reader needs the proof.

If Version 3 is what the reply means, it does not support the reply's use of the formula. It says the formula 'is not meant to compute a number' and that 'It is meant to enforce a hard rule', whose first step asks 'Does the proposed action risk eliminating life?' The review computes a number, and it applies that number to a definition in a physics paper.

The response's author states his position for this record: he abides by inescapable situational constraints, for example the Free Energy Principle in terms of persistence, and an ontological posture toward a higher power.

The Free Energy Principle is a useful comparison because it is a constraint of the kind the reply describes, and it does not do what the reply needs. Friston (2013) argues that life is 'an inevitable and emergent property of any (ergodic) random dynamical system that possesses a Markov blanket'; such systems appear to act 'to preserve their functional and structural integrity'. That describes what persists. It ranks no truths and assigns no office. Kirchhoff et al. (2018) note that the boundaries of living systems 'need not be co-extensive with the biophysical boundaries of a living organism', which sits closer to OPH's continuity than to a sharp line at life.

The form of the claim is a transcendental argument: X is 'a necessary condition for the possibility of' Y. Stroud's objection is that such an argument may show only what we must believe, 'but that S needn’t actually be true'. Even granting that inquiry needs living inquirers, it does not follow that the value of life is a premise of every theory inquirers produce.
