# OPH-REP-015: 'Disregarded the need for life to have an observer' restates the disputed point

## The reply says

> You postulated 'observer' while using life to make that statement and disregarded the need for life to have an observer.
>
> — [`reviews/oph-lfdi-57/correspondence/03-wishengrad-reply.txt:35`](https://github.com/docxology/WISLIM-audit/blob/main/reviews/oph-lfdi-57/correspondence/03-wishengrad-reply.txt#L35)

## Summary

OPH does not disregard the question. It takes it up directly and answers it the other way: record-keeping comes first, and living things are a very good kind of observer. Using a capacity to state a theory does not commit a theory to that capacity's supremacy.

## Assessment

The book addresses life by name, under 'What nobody has to add': 'Life. Obviously not, and this cuts both ways', and continues with 'a much better one of a kind'. Answering a question differently is not disregarding it.

'The need for life to have an observer' is the disputed claim, stated as a premise. Whether it holds depends on the definition of life (OPH-REP-010). A theory of weather can be written by people without making people meteorological objects; that the authors are alive is a fact about the authors. This is the charge tracked at OPH-REV-004 in a shorter form.

**Kind of claim:** `logic`: the inference does not follow as stated.
