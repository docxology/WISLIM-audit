# OPH-REP-001: 'Entirely separate from living agency' is not in the quoted fragments

> **Status: audit observation, not a required fix.** This is a tracked note on one
> claim in a reply. It is not a verdict on anyone's character, and the sender may

## The reply says

> This suggests that he is describing a structural or mathematical concept that he views as entirely separate from living agency.
>
> — [`reviews/oph-lfdi-57/correspondence/03-wishengrad-reply.txt:6`](https://github.com/docxology/WISLIM-audit/blob/main/reviews/oph-lfdi-57/correspondence/03-wishengrad-reply.txt#L6)

## Summary

The inference rests on two quoted fragments that do not carry it. Neither says 'separate', neither mentions living agency, and OPH's own text places living things and builders inside its account.

## Assessment

The step rests on two fragments of the response: 'without requiring biological life' and 'holds or fails with nothing running it' (reply line 5). The first says a property is not required. That is not separation: a definition of 'vehicle' that does not require wheels takes no wheels off any car. The second came from a sentence upstream has retired, and it was about the ordering of a fixed point, not about living agency (OPH-REP-009). No reason is given for the step from those fragments to 'entirely separate', and 'he views' reports an attitude the fragments do not state.

The next sentence also changes the object. The response said 'biological life'. The note says 'his definition explicitly removes the requirement for life'. Since LFDI's Life is substrate-neutral (OPH-REP-010), the difference matters.

OPH's text points the other way. Its closure hypothesis has observers 'build hardware that realizes that same history, including its builders', says 'Nothing lies outside this loop', and requires 'The builders' inference' to be among the 'operations available within the candidate history'. Its book says 'the arrival of living things in the universe did not introduce a new kind of physical entity. It introduced a much better one of a kind that had been there all along.' A reader may take that as a demotion of life, and may object to it. It is not separation. The book also says physics and ethics 'share a quantity'.

The note's closing sentence is fair: the two frameworks use different definitions of an observer. Different is not separate.

**Kind of claim:** `attribution`: attributes to the response or its author something the text does not say.
